# A Goose's Daydream Cut Short

I sometimes wonder what it would be like to be a goose sitting in the yellow afternoon rays. 
As the diagonal shadows streak across the neighborhood lawns, all seem to sit still as the fellow geese sway 
in the lake beside. Quabbling, as if that's the only thing to do. To eat, to fly, to bathe in this sustainable 
freshness of the expansive.

To be within a void of predators, of scarce conditions, to have servants clean up your waste, I feel one would 
be quite content in such a quiet, humane neighborhood. A neighborhood, though, that is built by blood, sweat, 
and societal progress of many thousands of years. I think it's fair to say that we would all gladly ravish the 
cornucopia, fly away into the dawning sunset, and lay waste unbeknowingly, like geese, like the wealthy elite, 
but would that even show a mark of your human essence? Would any revolt against pre-destined suffering be seen? 
Any sacrifice, any turmoil, any growth of trees whose shade you won't sit in?

I suppose it's important not to forget the serenity within, and not to let progress become the sole purpose of life, 
lest we be exploited by cruel hands, or by our own machinations, as we were once exploited by nature. Yet I can't 
say this door of intellectual progress and consciousness is one that should be easily closed.

Perhaps from time to time, one should become a goose again and bathe in life's rays, allowing enjoyment to be 
found within rather than some consumeristic product, but I believe our sweat and tears will ultimately ripple 
more in our life's meaning than some playful waddles.
