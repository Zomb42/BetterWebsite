# Some Crisp Pinecones

Petals wilt and flowers die, yet I don't think that makes them any less beautiful.

On a hike up the mountain trail, I ran into a gray, ashy pine tree at rest. 
I had been thinking about my sore legs and how far the summit still was, so I nearly stepped past it without 
a look. Perhaps it was old age, or, upon closer inspection, probably an unfortunate victim of a fast fire, 
but it lay there, blocking the path onwards amongst a forest of dark green. Seemingly charred and far from 
life, it stubbornly still radiated a sense of elegance. Its burnt pinecones lay in full bloom, like flowers 
ornamenting a gothic statue. Not rotting at all, but rather somewhat content in its tragedy. Its seeds, 
displaced all around and flung out during its death, reconfirm that story. Would this tree still like to be 
standing? Probably, but the flame heated its cones until they popped open like popcorn, releasing seeds to 
corners much farther than they would have reached otherwise. In that way, I suppose there's beauty in its sacrifice.

I carefully climbed over its grave and continued trotting on the dirt path ahead.
