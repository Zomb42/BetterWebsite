#I Like Titles

Bit of a shorter train of thought here. 

For some reason, one of my favorite parts about writing is to come up with the title for a piece.
While it is the first impression of the reader, and in some sense, the most important few words of 
the entire piece, I don't really feel much pressure at all. It seems like its simplicity yet 
unlimitedness has a strange delectable appeal to me. As opposed to normal text in a piece that has to 
be well formatted to fit the grammatic structure, make sense meaningfully, and play along with the 
rythmic flow, a title just seems like a piece of dessert that somehow choose at the end of their multi-layered
course of word. There are just less requirements needed for what pretty words to pick. 

Also opposed to normal words, it seems like titles don't need to be as specific. Of course, a title should
fit the piece at hand, but its inherent limitations in length leaves a lot of generality and vagueness naturally.
This vagueness isn't that great when produced from normal text, as I believe good text should be clear and 
make sense (though emotionally charged is fine), but I think it really adds to a title's mystique. The 
reader should know the general idea about what the piece is on, but that open vagueness can lead to fun 
surprises or connections that the reader originally didn't think about when considering.
