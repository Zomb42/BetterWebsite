# I Like Titles

Bit of a less poetic train of thought here. 

For some reason, one of my favorite parts about writing is to come up with the title for a piece.
While it is the first impression of the reader, and in some sense, the most important few words of 
the entire piece, I don't really feel much pressure at all. It seems like its simplicity yet 
unlimitedness has a strange delectable appeal to me. As opposed to normal text in a piece that has to 
be well formatted to fit the grammatic structure, make sense meaningfully, and play along with the 
rythmic flow, a title just seems like a piece of dessert that somehow choose at the end of their multi-layered
course of word. There are just less requirements needed for what pretty words to pick. 

Also opposed to normal words, it seems like titles don't need to be as specific. Of course, a title should
fit the piece at hand, but its inherent limitations in length leaves a lot of generality and vagueness naturally.
This vagueness isn't that great when produced from normal text, as I believe good text should be clear and 
make sense (though emotionally charged is fine), but I think it really adds to a title's mystique. The 
reader should know the general idea about what the piece is on, but that open vagueness can lead to fun 
surprises or connections that the reader originally didn't think about when considering.

Consider the song (essentially a lyrical prose) "Hate That I Made You Love Me" by Ariana Grande. It's title 
is vague, and I would argue to a point where it kind of is an issue grammatically. Like, it doens't just accept
its incompleteness and rather tries to mask it with more words. It's imcompleteness nature also begs for 
someone to fill in the messing noun at the beginning, but depending on the word "I" or "You", it transforms the 
proposed meaning of the piece. "I hate that I made you love me" seems much more regretful than "You hate I made 
you love me" which seems a bit cheeky yet also twisted in morality. 

I guess it can be thought of that titles are somewhat similar to meaingful symbols as they project meaning, 
but it can have its meaning altered as the user's perspective changing with different users as or if the same
user experiences different parts of the their life/story. Unlike perhaps a cool symbolic object, like a shark tooth
necklace for instance, I feel like a title can't really be a story of itself (unlike the origin story of the shark tooth necklace).
It points to stories and meaning, but that's all that it can be. To use programming context, it's kind of like a pointer in C that just refers 
to a memory, perhaps a file, but it's not a substantial block of memory by itself. It's just a couple of words.

Maybe it's because of the multidisciplinary nature of it, but I think I like titles.
