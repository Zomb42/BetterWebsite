#A Stained Impression

"You know, you have to really appreciate the years here. Before you know it, it's all going to be over."

Before you know it,

it's all,

going,

to be,

over.

Even though it was two and a half years ago, it seemed like it was only yesterday when my freshman self kneeled 
beside the Grotto in the coolness of the spring eve and admired the glowing candlelight. The school year had 
just passed and the blur of memories of the chaotic year had finally set in as "the past." It was quite beautiful, 
in that moment, to reflect upon everything as we stared upon the glowing flames, carrying ashes and smoke to the 
heavens above, like how our past year had burned away in such beautiful warmth. The friends, the embarrassments, 
the love, the misfortune, all felt so real and specific but at the same time so interconnected. Their present 
reality flickered away as fiery specks of dust, charring the memories into our existence.

Quiet murmurs were exchanged, but in that moment, one specific line, by an awfully drunk friend, really stained 
my impression of the college years.

"You know, you have to really appreciate the years here. Before you know it, it's all going to be over."

In retrospect, I think such a line was perhaps the least significant of all the chatter, as that friend's 
drunkenness had surpassed the levels of basic remembrance, but in some sense, I feel like that is what makes 
it special. It was merely a casual truth, flung out of the unconscious, by a lip taking a breath from sips of 
mysteriously concocted liquor.

He was just a sophomore, reflecting on some nostalgia to me, a mere freshman, but before I knew it, we walked 
past each other a handful more times, cheered for each other in a few cultural performances, sweated in winning 
a competition together, celebrated at another friend's wedding, and then he was gone, graduated in the late ides 
of spring.

I'm now left, a husk of my past self, staring down the same fate of graduation barreling by.

These seeds that he loosely spilled back then have blossomed in full fragrance, and I do think they call us 
to appreciate the preciousness of time, but I'm not sure what that best entails in practice.

Knowing our time is fleeting, is it best to grasp it tightly, burning brighter than before, then let the 
cold air of passing rip it from our arms?

Maybe one could say that's the best way to appreciate something, and I don't think you can argue a better 
way to say goodbye to a dying mother lying on a hospital bed, yet I feel one thing special about life is that 
it's not just filled with these powerful moments but rather mostly of the mundane.

Dancing on the night of commencement should be required, but in the quiet afternoons within, I feel appreciation 
of college life can still be done without leaving the entire fanfare until the end.

Grasp the friends and memories college provides, but also the rare opportunities for growth ahead. To appreciate 
a fleeting sensation is to recognize its end, and that what we plant now can bloom long after this initial chance passes.

Those long nights spent working, practicing, and studying aren't pretty, but I don't think they need to be. 
It's dirty work to prepare a garden, kneeling in the soil for new flowers that stem from a single bloom.

Perhaps some of the new flowers end up trampled, but I suppose the best way to appreciate fragility is to water 
the future carefully and admire the blooms that do arise. The candles I lit at the Grotto two and a half years 
ago burned out long ago, yet I still feel the warmth of his words on my dusty hands. In that way, such a few words 
left a stained impression on me.
